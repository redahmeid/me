My colleague Dave Sammut and I wrote a piece for the Equal Experts blog on one of the most common decisions in technology strategy: whether to build or buy. Our argument is that it is rarely a binary choice. Two similar companies can reach opposite conclusions and both be right, because context decides it: timing, capabilities, risk appetite and how the organisation actually works. The piece covers the homework to do first (framing the problem, mapping the architecture that really runs in production and identifying the non-negotiables), an honest check on how unique you really are, and a strategic alignment test: are you trying to change the game, or win the current one? It also looks at whether your skills, processes, team structure and appetite for change can support what you choose, how to drive vendor conversations instead of letting vendors define your needs, and a handful of heuristics, from "decide what you want to own" to "build less, buy more". Most good outcomes end up as a mix of the two.
